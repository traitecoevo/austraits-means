# Roadmap: modelled species means and community means from climate

This is the planning document for `austraits-means`. It records what the repository delivers, the decisions taken and why, the milestones and the issues that reach them, and the invariants a future edit could break. Keep it current or retire it; do not start a second plan beside it. Issue numbers are on the [AusTraits board](https://github.com/orgs/traitecoevo/projects/9) (view "means").

Last revised: 26 November 2026.

## Products

Re-scoped 24 November 2026 (decisions below). This repository publishes one product, in a Zenodo record of its own, for a short list of traits; terms are defined in `terminology.md`:

1. **Modelled means**: per species × trait, the model-estimated geometric mean at the centre of the species' climate distribution and an average location, from the location model (`R/model/spread_rtmb.R`; 25 November 2026).
2. **Modelled community statistics**, in the same record: for a location, predicted from its climate alone, the woody and non-woody **community mean** (mean trait across species) and **community range** (spread of the species' trait means there). They are a function of climate, which can be evaluated at any location; they are not maps of the records. They come from the same location model, from each 50 km cell's climate alone (`community_stats`, 25 November 2026).

With them, as a draft (25 November 2026): **forecasting tables**, so users can forecast without the model. Per species, the three numbers that give its trait value at any climate (mean at the climate centre, the two within-species slopes) and their covariance; per trait and growth form, the variances to add for a new site or record, and what forecasting a species without trait data needs (the growth form's five coefficients and their covariance, the species spread and slope SDs), for the 22,632 species with a climate centre and a growth form; and the community statistics on a regular climate grid (`R/process/forecast.R`, `outputs/forecast/<build>/`). `reports/forecasting.qmd` shows their use and checks them against the data.

Not published from here:

- **Observed means** (the simple species means) are published by austraits.build with each AusTraits release and its Zenodo record ([austraits.build#899](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits.build/pull/899)). This repository reads them.
- No community layers other than the statistics above. The three 10 km layers (`observed_local`, `observed_presence`, `modelled`), their native-only variants and comparisons were dropped on 25 November 2026 (decision below). The benchmark for the community range is the spread among the species recorded together in a cell, computed from the location model's own records (`docs/local-range-model.qmd`).
- The **portal-method observed means for AusTraits 7.0.0** were retired on 24 November 2026, unpublished, and their code, reproduction check, method document and Zenodo packaging were removed on 25 November 2026 (decision below; in git history up to `415a786`).

The trait list is open until the location-model prototype has been checked trait by trait (Later work). How the new record is named and versioned is open.

## Decisions and their reasons

Dated decisions, newest first. Earlier decisions from the refactor plan are kept below them.

### 26 November 2026

- **The data basis is the AusTraits 8.0.0 release** ([10.5281/zenodo.24543275](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24543275), published 26 November 2026; austraits.build `489360f0`; 503 datasets, 2,450,777 trait records), replacing the development build 7.0.0.9000 at `d535de9` (470 datasets). The database and its observed means are taken from the Zenodo record; the pipeline checks both against the record's MD5s and writes the version, commit and DOI into the product metadata (`data/austraits/README.md`). The products are under `outputs/*/8.0.0/`, following the versioning rule in `terminology.md`. Nothing else changed: the APC pin (2026-07-30), the GBIF download and medoids, the model, the trait list and the growth-form rule. The APC release is not re-pinned, because the crosswalk realigns the release's names to the pin whatever release they were built with; 89.6% of the trait species have a medoid (90.0% before). A later release is a change of five values in `_targets.R`.
- **What the change of data did, 7.0.0.9000 against 8.0.0** (same code, one rebuild each):
  - Eight of the nine traits barely move. Records with a medoid change by at most 3% (wood density +7%); the modelled means of species in both correlate at 0.993 or more on the log scale, with a median absolute shift of at most 0.03; a cell's community mean shifts by a median of at most 0.06 log units, except non-woody leaf area (−0.19).
  - **Seed mass changes materially, and its earlier statistics are superseded.** Four seed-bank datasets new in 8.0.0 (`WA_Seed_Centre_2026`, `SA_Seed_Conservation_Centre_2026`, `ANBG_2026`, `Tas_Seed_Conservation_Centre_2026`) take the records with a medoid from 17,384 to 30,728 and the species from 1,939 to 6,008. Fitted species rise from 214 to 665: woody 187 to 440, non-woody 27 to 225. The non-woody fit had rested on 27 species, with 2,546 of 3,351 cells outside the climate of its records; now 13 cells are. Its community mean falls by a median 2.06 log units (about 8-fold), and the woody community SD is a median 0.78 of what it was.
  - Leaf area: the `Laliberte_2012` values were corrected in the release (10 to 50 times smaller for the species checked), which moves six Western Australian species' modelled means down by 1.5 to 2.2 log units.
  - The same three growth forms are dropped (non-woody wood density, leaf N and P per area). All nine location fits converge, and the forecasting checks hold (94% intervals hold 92–97% of site means for species treated as unmeasured).
- Numbers in the dated entries below, in Later work and in `docs/local-range-model.qmd` were measured on the development build 7.0.0.9000 and are not recomputed; for seed mass they no longer describe the product.

### 25 November 2026

Decided by Daniel Falster:

- **All portal-method material is removed** (Daniel, 25 November 2026): the code (`R/means/observed_means_portal.R`, `compare_portal.R`), its targets, reproduction test and method document, the portal-vs-geometric comparison, the core-trait list in `data/portal/`, and the Zenodo draft code written for its deposit (`R/release/zenodo.R`). The product was retired the day before, and nothing else read them; they are in git history up to `415a786`. A deposit for the modelled means will be scripted afresh when that record is opened. Supersedes the 24 November note that the code stays in the pipeline unmaintained.
- **The trait list is all nine traits, each for both growth forms where it can be fitted.** Plant height, LMA, leaf area, leaf N and P per dry mass and seed mass for woody and non-woody plants; wood density and leaf N and P per area for woody plants only (below). Sparse traits and growth forms are kept where the model can fit them, and their sparseness shows as wide intervals: the community statistics now carry intervals for parameter uncertainty. A growth form the model cannot fit is dropped, not propped up by borrowing variances from the other growth form (Daniel, 25 November 2026): it needs 5 species and records in at least 10 locations, and if the fit still fails to converge the smallest growth form is dropped and the trait refitted (`fit_location_target()`). That drops non-woody wood density (2 species, 2 cells), leaf P per area (7 species, 3 cells; did not converge) and leaf N per area (8 species at 4 cells: it converged, but 7 of the species were measured at the same 2–3 sites, so location effects and within-species slopes could not be separated and its species means swung far from the data, Ptilotus 4.2 against 0.75 observed). The next smallest growth form, non-woody leaf P per mass, has 37 species in 16 cells. Variance parameters estimated at zero are treated as known when uncertainty is propagated (their log-scale standard errors are meaningless). Cells whose climate lies outside a trait's records are flagged (`extrapolated`); a straight line on the log scale is assumed there, which the intervals do not cover. Alternative considered: a core list of six, holding back seed mass and the leaf P traits.
- **The community range gets a sorting factor.** Species with the same climate centre sort among locations, so species that grow together are more alike than the model's species SD implies (tested with the species effects and GBIF co-occurrence, Later work). The species-spread term of the community variance is scaled by k, the ratio of the within-cell to the expected variance of the species effects, per trait and growth form, capped at 1 (`estimate_sorting_factors()`). It does not vary with climate yet. Alternative considered: leaving the range as the model gives it.
- **No genus effect.** Dropped; it was an option for co-sampled congeners. The new Zenodo record stays on hold.
- **The pooled models' only role is as the comparison that shows they are inadequate.** Everything else reads the location model: the modelled means and the working per-trait exports, the fixed-effect summary (per trait and growth form), the per-trait figures (woody and non-woody apart), the figure set, the baseline comparison and the analysis report. The models are named by what they pool (`terminology.md`, Model names): the **pooled** model (growth forms and locations pooled; `tmb_fit`), the **locations-pooled** model (`woody_fit`) and the **location model**, and `pooled_vs_location` compares them step by step, because separating growth forms and separating locations change the species means and the community spread in different ways. The BRMS backend fits only the pooled model, so it is no route to the product.
- **The three 10 km community layers are dropped, replaced by one set of community statistics from the location model.** The modelled layer came from the locations-pooled model, which overstates the local spread (woody LMA 1.62, wood density 1.97 against observed); the observed local layer's role as a benchmark is now filled from the location model's own records; the observed presence layer is the kind of observed map the 24 November decision does not publish; every layer had an all-species group that decision ruled out; the native-only variants tested products that are gone. That removed 33 of 93 targets and about 900 lines. Kept, because predicting from climate alone needs them: GBIF presence per cell, the GBIF-to-species map, cell climate, the woody lookup. The statistics are on the location model's 50 km cells, not the 10 km grid of #20. Alternative considered: keeping the layers as unpublished diagnostics.
- **One model for every purpose: the location model gives the modelled means as well as the community statistics.** Its species means and the community range then come from one variance split and cannot disagree; a species measured at one unusual site no longer carries that site's effect; and there is one production model to maintain. The modelled mean becomes the species' mean at its climate centre and an average location (`terminology.md`), with a delta-method interval checked against glmmTMB (with constant spread the two agree to 1e-6, once glmmTMB's new-location variance is taken out). It loses few species: those without a woody or non-woody class (21 for height, 1 for leaf area) and the non-woody species of traits with fewer than 20 of them (leaf N and P per area, 8 and 7; wood density, 2); they fall back to observed means in the combined table. The pooled model stays in the pipeline as a diagnostic (fixed-effect summary, per-trait figures, baseline comparison, analysis report) until those are moved or retired. The BRMS backend applies only to the pooled model. Alternative considered: keeping the pooled model for species means and the location model for community statistics.
- **The community range has climate-dependent spread for every trait, with log species SD linear in the species' rainfall and temperature centres, shrunk by empirical Bayes; no quadratic terms.** One model for every trait, and the data decide each slope: shrinkage sets it to 0 where they don't support it (LMA, leaf N per mass, leaf P per mass), which is constant spread. The quadratic is dropped for three reasons. On the log scale it grows or collapses fast outside the species' centres, and the product predicts from climate anywhere. It was supported only for leaf area and height, and adds little to their linear terms (AIC −190 against −166, −93 against −75). And one slope per climate axis is easy to report and defend. Alternatives considered: a per-trait choice between constant and climate-dependent spread; keeping the shrunk quadratic, which the data choose for leaf area and height. Evidence: `docs/local-range-model.qmd`, Later work.

### 24 November 2026

Product re-scope, decided by Daniel Falster:

- **Simple (observed) means are published by austraits.build with each AusTraits release and its Zenodo record, not here.** They are a direct summary of the release, and the API and portal can then serve one copy. Supersedes the 14 October decision that derived products share one "AusTraits species means" deposit.
- **The portal-method observed means for AusTraits 7.0.0 are retired, unpublished** ([#6](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/6), [#10](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/10) closed). The successor method ships with the release, so a separate archive of the 7.0.0 portal values adds little. Their code and reproduction check stay in the pipeline, unmaintained. Alternatives considered: finishing the #10 deposit as a one-off archive; handing the table to austraits.build to sit with 7.0.0.
- **Modelled means are a separate product, for far fewer traits, in a new Zenodo record.** Which traits is decided after the location-model prototype, by how well each trait supports a community range. Evidence so far is in Later work.
- **No observed community layers are published: there are too few records.** A trait record collected in a cell is rare: 72–459 sites have 5 or more species measured together for the eight leaf, seed and wood traits (Later work). Spatial output is predicted from climate by the trait model instead. The observed layers already built stay as unpublished diagnostics, because their within-cell spread is the benchmark for the modelled community range. Supersedes the 19 October decision on three community products. Alternative considered: removing them.
- **The community statistics are, for a location and from its climate, the community mean and the community range, for woody and non-woody plants separately** (`terminology.md`). There is no "all species" statistic: mixing the two groups needs each location's woody share, which comes from occurrence data.
- **Community variance is between species only, within one location and within one growth form** (woody or non-woody), so it excludes the difference between the groups as well as variation within species.
- **The community range comes from a model with location effects, never from the pooled species variance.** The current model pools all records from the same climate, so the spread among its species includes how locations with the same climate differ; the spread at one location is smaller (measured: 0.59–0.92 of it, Later work). The next modelling step is a model that separates the two ([#41](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/41)).

Observed means:

- **Signed traits get an arithmetic observed mean** (traits.use#51). A trait whose definition allows values below zero (water potentials, temperatures, isotope ratios; 43 in the development build) has no geometric mean, so its `value` is the arithmetic mean of the same groups, labelled `method = arithmetic`. Alternative considered: leaving such traits without an observed mean, as the first version did (23 traits had none).
- **The calculation moves to traits.use** as `summarise_trait_means()` (traits.use#51), after Lizzy Wenk's review: usable on any traits.build database or subset, every trait by default, with `by` for further grouping (AusInverTraits splits by sex). austraits.build#900 calls it.

- **The geometric observed means ship with each AusTraits release, built in austraits.build, not here** ([austraits.build#899](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits.build/pull/899)). `post_export()` in austraits.build's `R/post_export.R`, which `traits.build::build_post_export()` runs from `create_release()`, writes `austraits-<version>-observed-means.parquet`, `.csv.gz` and `-dictionary.csv` into the release. ### 19 October 2026

- **Community statistics are presence-only: every species present in a unit counts equally**, so they are the *community mean* and *community variance*, never CWM or CWV ([#18](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/18)). Abundance-weighted statistics from survey plots were considered; they cover far fewer sites and species.
- **Community layers use a 10 × 10 km equal-area grid, as Andrew et al. 2025** (Lambert Azimuthal Equal Area, `+proj=laea +lat_0=-25.2744 +lon_0=133.7751 +x_0=0 +y_0=0 +datum=WGS84 +units=m`), **at least 10 species with a mean per cell and group, and GBIF occurrence points for presence** ([#20](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/20)). Range maps from species distribution models were considered as the occurrence source.
- **A species' woodiness and growth form are their most likely value as the AusTraits data portal computes it** (master's categorical summary: each space-separated value counted once per record, the most frequent kept, ties joined); the geometric observed means' `value_mode` is that same value ([#19](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/19)).
- **Woody: `woodiness_detailed` woody or woody_like_stem (palms, cycads, grass trees); non-woody: herbaceous and the other intermediate categories (semi_woody, woody_base, woody_like_inflorescence, woody_root); then growth-form keywords; species still unassigned are left out of the woody and non-woody layers, with no fallback to the coarse `woodiness` trait** ([#19](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/19)). Unassigned species are few (23 in the development build) and unclear cases.
- *(Superseded 24 November 2026: none of these is published; they stay as diagnostics.)* **Three community products, replacing the first draft's variants** ([#20](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/20), [#28](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/28)): *observed, local records*, where each species' records in a cell are averaged and then the species, with no model; *observed, GBIF presence*, the species present in a cell each given its geometric observed mean; and *modelled*, the per-trait model's net average for the cell, b0 + b_within·x + (b_between − b_within)·m̄, from the cell's climate x and the mean climate centre m̄ of the species present, using no species means. The first keeps values tied to where traits were measured; the second gives coverage; the third averages over between- and within-species effects. All three are built for woody and non-woody species separately, and for all species. The modelled product is split through a woodiness term in each trait's model (woody and non-woody intercepts and climate slopes, species random effects shared), so the layer for all species mixes the two by their species in the cell; without it, the model, fitted mostly on woody species, could not follow the woody share of a cell. Separate models per group were the alternative; the shared random effects help the groups with few species. A group with fewer than 20 fitted species gets no modelled layer (non-woody leaf N and P per area, wood density).

GBIF licenses a download as its most restrictive record, and both downloads used are CC BY-NC 4.0. In the October 2026 download the CC BY-NC records are 12.3% (3.36 million, mostly iNaturalist research-grade observations, NSW weeds and the ACT Wildlife Atlas); dropping them leaves 603 of 27,730 species names with no records, of which only 49 have a trait mean. ## Invariants

Things a future edit could break without noticing:

- A community range or community variance is never computed from the pooled species variance of a model without location effects: that includes how locations with the same climate differ, and overstates the spread at one location.
- Community statistics are given per growth form (woody, non-woody); the variance is between species within one form and one location.
- Value-type rules are per dataset: datasets that carry only minimum/maximum records (the AVH height dataset) enter the modelled means by an explicit rule, not by the default raw/mean/median filter.
- Centring is on the GBIF medoid, never on the trait-sample mean.
- Both species estimates are geometric means from the geometric method onward, except the observed mean of a signed trait, which is arithmetic and labelled `method = arithmetic` (24 November 2026); otherwise an arithmetic mean is always a separately labelled column.
- Column names follow `terminology.md`: `estimate_type` and `method`, never a bare `estimate` or `geom_mean`.
- Community statistics are unweighted unless the weighting decision says otherwise; unweighted values are never called CWM or CWV.
- Use "mean", never "average", in code, exports, docs and metadata.

- **Location model for the community range** ([#41](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/41), feeding [#38](https://github.com/traitecoevo/austraits-means/issues/38)). Extend the woodiness model (`fit_woodiness_model()`) with a location random effect and a species × location term, variances per growth form:

  ```
  log_value ~ 0 + g + g:(P_mn + T_mn + P_c + T_c)
            + diag(0 + g | taxon_name) + (0 + P_c | taxon_name) + (0 + T_c | taxon_name)
            + diag(0 + g | location) + (1 | taxon_name:location)
  ```

  `g` is the growth form and `location` a coarse equal-area cell, 50 km by default (coarse so that a location pools several studies; see the result below). The location variance (same climate, different place) is left out of the community range. The species variance, the species × location variance and the spread of co-occurring species' climate centres around the location's climate go into it. The prototype (`R/model/local_range.R`, run by `R/scripts/run_location_prototype.R`, reported in `docs/local-range-model.qmd`) is kept out of the product targets until it passes all four checks:
  1. A simulation in which species sort among locations: the model recovers the known community SD, and the current model overstates it.
  2. The model's community SD agrees with the observed within-location SD, per growth form.
  3. Likelihood and AIC with and without the location terms, and the convergence and fit time for height (~320k records, ~29k cells).
  4. The shift in the modelled species means, old against new.

  Risk to test, not assume: one location term covers both species sorting among locations and species responding to a location; if sorting dominates, the split between species and location variance is biased.

  Evidence (24 November 2026, `austraits_climate` target, natural-log SDs). "Within a location" is the SD among species' means at sites with 5 or more species. "Pooled" is the SD of species means of residuals from a climate-only regression, a crude benchmark not yet split by growth form:

  | trait | locations with ≥5 species | within a location | pooled, given climate | ratio |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | leaf_mass_per_area | 459 sites | 0.45 | 0.76 | 0.59 |
  | leaf_P_per_dry_mass | 178 sites | 0.47 | 0.70 | 0.67 |
  | leaf_N_per_dry_mass | 259 sites | 0.37 | 0.49 | 0.76 |
  | leaf_area | 324 sites | 1.45 | 1.86 | 0.78 |
  | leaf_P_per_area | 72 sites | 0.41 | 0.51 | 0.80 |
  | wood_density | 120 sites | 0.21 | 0.25 | 0.84 |
  | leaf_N_per_area | 101 sites | 0.40 | 0.45 | 0.89 |
  | plant_height | 10,924 cells (10 km) | 1.19 | 1.30 | 0.92 |
  | seed_dry_mass | 53 sites | 2.19 | 2.40 | 0.91 |

  Site, exact-coordinate and 10 km-cell groupings give near-identical SDs for the eight leaf, seed and wood traits. Height is 99% herbarium specimens with one record per location, so it has replication only at the cell grain.

  - **Check 2 mostly passes.** Model-to-observed community SD ratios (corrected 24 November 2026 in 3c2156f: earlier figures left out the species × location variance and were 0.03–0.06 low):
    - location model: 1.01–1.33, mostly 1.10–1.25, against 1.00–1.97 for the pooled model;
    - the exception is wood density at 1.57.
  - **A 10–25% overstatement remains, and it is mostly in cells with few species.** Cells with 5–9 species give ratios of 1.16–1.95 (leaf P per area 3.19, from 9 cells); cells with 20 or more give 0.89–1.18. Height, from herbarium specimens rather than targeted studies, stays nearly flat (1.16 → 1.10). This points to studies co-sampling similar species, so the observed spread is too small in those cells, rather than to the model being too wide. Suggestive, not proof: species-rich cells also differ in other ways.
  - **Spread as a function of climate** (Daniel asked, 24 November 2026). Two versions, both in the notebook:
    - binned (glmmTMB): one species variance per climate quartile of species;
    - continuous (RTMB, `R/model/spread_rtmb.R`): log species SD linear in the species' rainfall and temperature centres, per growth form (decision of 25 November 2026; a quadratic version came first). With constant spread it reproduces glmmTMB to 1e-7; on simulated data it recovers a known spread slope.

    1. **The climate slopes are shrunk (25 November 2026).** Free, the first (quadratic) version overfitted small groups: non-woody seed mass (27 species) got a hump from 0.02 to 2.5 that the binned estimates (0.8–1.7) do not show, and non-woody leaf P per mass and dry-end wood density went to about 0. The climate coefficients now get a prior b ~ N(0, τ²), τ shared by the growth forms and estimated by empirical Bayes from the free fit; the model is then refitted with that ridge penalty (`fit_spread_shrunk()`). Linear, shrunk, against constant spread:
       - AIC: leaf area −166, height −75, wood density −43, seed mass −29, leaf N per area −5, the others 0 to −1. Only the first four have a log Bayes factor above 2 (13–77).
